# Asia's Past — standing storytelling standard

User explicitly prefers Ottoman Days / Lost Legacy-style immersive second-person storytelling. Apply to future scripts without requiring the user to repeat it.

- Deliver an actual situation in the first sentence, designed to land within approximately three seconds. Measure actual narration before claiming timing. No guarantee of CTR, retention or monetization.
- Establish identity, place, period and stakes early. Use names/years where they explain a development; no ornamental fact lists.
- Build connected scenes: situation, pressure, response, consequence. An opening question or problem must receive a payoff.
- Simple spoken English. No essay introduction, chapter headings in clean narration, repeated thesis paragraphs or explanation after the scene already conveys the point.
- Research stays in description/notes. Do not put “the account describes,” author biographies, evidence audits or “we cannot invent” commentary in ordinary narration. Material historical uncertainty must still be handled honestly, using concise qualification, a reconstruction disclosure or a clearly bounded setting.
- Never cure weak evidence by removing attribution and stating uncertain material as verified fact. Verify period/place, retain appropriate uncertainty and exclude unsupported claims.
- Separate a composite from a named biography. Connective scenes and reactions may be openly reconstructed; do not invent named victims, private quotations, abuse, contracts, debts, punishments or deaths as documented facts.
- Do not combine Edo geisha, modern Kyoto maiko, twentieth-century hot-spring workers and Korean gisaeng into one supposedly typical life. A shift in period/place needs to be explicit.
- Keep harsh realities visible through actions and consequences, without making every guest evil or every colleague a rival. Include competence and ordinary relationships.
- Read supplied reference scripts when revising storytelling. Learn structure; do not copy their expression or adopt unsourced dramatic claims.
- Write years in full for voiceover; correct historical spellings for screen labels, regardless of TTS phonetics. Keep clean Word and TXT copies separate from evidence/production notes.
- Visuals: mostly accurate illustrations; every concrete noun/action should be pictured. Plain white/ivory text cards, simple dark narrow lettering, no drawings/decorations on text cards. Names, dates, places and key terms appear where introduced.
- Editing: sharp 16:9, purposeful hard cuts, no automatic zooms/transitions, no repeated filler portraits. Latest audio preference: soft music/selective effects beneath narration. HyperFrames preview and user approval before export.
- Runtime must reflect actual word count and likely delivery. Previously supplied audio ran around 181 words/minute. Do not call a short text a measured 22-minute video or pad it with repetition; give honest estimates.
