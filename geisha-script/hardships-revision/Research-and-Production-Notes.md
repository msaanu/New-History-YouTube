# Life as a Geisha: hardship-focused storytelling revision

## Scope
The narrator is a composite adult geisha. Main scenes illustrate reported earlier Yoshiwara controls, rather than a documented individual or a single precisely dated day. The suspension scene is explicitly hypothetical. No actual accusation, customer, romantic relationship, assault, illness, sale price or debt balance is invented as a historical fact.

Use a title such as **Why Life as a Geisha Wasn't What You Imagine** or **Life as a Geisha in Edo's Pleasure Quarter**. The description must identify retrospective evidence and reconstruction. Do not imply all geisha across Japan lived under these particular regulations.

The inspected text narrates older rules after the establishment of the Yoshiwara kembansho in 1779, and distinguishes those rules from practices current in the author's own era.

7. Music/dance preparation and conversational work: broad activities in Mitford/Bacon, with illustrative choreography and interactions. No attested daily timetable.
8. Shamisen three strings and plectrum: Inouye's physical description used narrowly, not as evidence of an Edo work schedule.
9. Adult-arranged child purchase and liability paper: Mitford. The narration explicitly calls this an account around the end of the era; not a proved childhood of our protagonist, not an original contract personally inspected.
10. Sitting closely, suspicion and one-to-three-day suspension: De Becker. The imagined next evening is explicitly hypothetical, not a documented punishment case. Exact lost income is not calculated.
11. Guest dancing with fan: Silver's 1867 illustrated pleasure-party description. Inserted as a similar kind of moment, not claimed as the exact Yoshiwara party described elsewhere.
12. Fee allocation: De Becker describes registry/performer division; Mitford describes fee to troupe head plus separate gift. Narration keeps these as different reported arrangements. Do not merge into one budget or assert no wages.
13. Social judgement: Bacon is evidence of the writer's attitude, not the women's conduct.
14. Meiji education epilogue: Stanley's accessible abstract only; timeline explicitly moves to 1870s/1880s and other women.

## Description-ready evidence disclosure
This episode reconstructs an adult geisha's working world in Edo's Yoshiwara. Its central restrictions are reported retrospectively in Joseph De Becker's history, written after Edo ended; they are not presented as universal rules for all geisha. Other accounts around the end of Edo and in Meiji provide context. The narrator is a composite. Scenes, reactions and the hypothetical evening under suspension are dramatized, not a particular woman's recorded biography. The closing section explicitly moves into Meiji. Geisha and courtesans are distinct occupations. Sources contain prejudices that the episode does not endorse.
