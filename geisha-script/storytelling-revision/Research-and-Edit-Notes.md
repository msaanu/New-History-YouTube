# Geisha storytelling revision — research and production notes

## What this version is
A second-person composite working day in the entertainment world around the end of Edo, followed by an explicitly later Meiji epilogue. The protagonist is an anonymous adult, not an authenticated biographical subject. Practice, dressing, arrival, glances, the order of performances, the conversational hesitation and departure are dramatized connective scenes. No private dialogue is quoted. No fictional sale price, age, patron, abuse, punishment, illness, death or rescue is asserted.

**Period limitation:** the detailed Yoshiwara payment, supervision and childhood-purchase account is Mitford, published in 1871 and using an 1869 guide. That is early Meiji. The opening discloses accounts just after Edo; this is not evidence that each detail applied everywhere throughout Edo. For strict pre-1868 historical packaging, these arrangements still need period-specific corroboration. Recommended accurate framing: “Life as a Geisha in Old Japan,” or explicitly “around the end of Edo.” Do not market it as the documented day of a named Edo geisha.

## Visual and editing direction
Mostly original scene illustrations. Text cards should be occasional plain ivory/white backgrounds with uncluttered dark narrow handwritten-style lettering. No illustrations, flourishes, textures or ornaments on text cards. Keep source citations in the description, not throughout narration.

Show the noun and action spoken: three shamisen strings and a plectrum; adult hands negotiating training paperwork while the child stays nonsexualized; kimono adjustment; two or more performers departing; performance exchanged between women; a sake bottle and cup; guest with a fan; two different payment destinations; instrument put down at home; clearly later educational scenes.

Use period-appropriate adult geisha clothing independently checked against dated references. No automatic modern Kyoto maiko template, oiran procession costume or Korean dress. Do not invent exact period contract text or show a legible forged historical document. Generic paper is an illustration, not a reproduced archival record.

Names/terms: geisha, Edo, Tokyo, Yoshiwara, shamisen, oiran, sake, hana. A brief label belongs at its first useful mention, not before narration introduces it. Meiji epilogue should be visibly separated with “MEIJI ERA” and “1870s” / “1880s” where spoken. Do not place Meiji schools in the earlier evening.

Cut when meaning changes, not on a fixed timer. Use detailed views of objects and action as well as wider surroundings; avoid repeating the same face while new ideas are spoken. Keep performances long enough to understand. No automatic pans, zooms or transitions. Music and selective effects stay below narration, per latest user instruction. This deliverable contains writing only; no images/audio/video generated and no render made.

## Description-ready disclosure
This episode follows an adult composite geisha through a reconstructed working day around the end of the Edo era. Scenes and reactions are dramatized; the narrator is not a named historical woman's diary. Our sources include accounts written just after Edo ended, so the episode does not claim that every arrangement applied across all places and periods. The closing section moves explicitly into Meiji. Geisha and courtesans were distinct occupations, and the video does not treat them as interchangeable.
